# Market Segmentation Framework: 50 Distinct Segments

## Segmentation Methodology

Segments are defined across four dimensions:
1. **Industry Vertical** (10 categories)
2. **Company Size** (5 tiers)
3. **Geographic Region** (3 zones)
4. **Use Case Intensity** (3 levels)

Total permutations: 10 × 5 = 50 primary segments (consolidated model)

---

## Segment Definitions

### Industry Verticals (10)

1. **Technology & Software** - High digital maturity, price-insensitive, early adopters
2. **Financial Services** - Regulatory requirements, security-focused, high willingness-to-pay
3. **Healthcare & Life Sciences** - Compliance-driven, long sales cycles, high retention
4. **E-commerce & Retail** - Cost-sensitive, seasonal demand, high churn risk
5. **Professional Services** - Small teams, project-based usage, moderate budgets
6. **Manufacturing & Supply Chain** - Traditional buyers, integration-heavy, price-sensitive
7. **Media & Entertainment** - Creative workflows, variable team sizes, moderate budgets
8. **Education & Non-Profit** - Budget-constrained, mission-driven, high price elasticity
9. **Government & Public Sector** - Procurement processes, compliance, long contracts
10. **SMB/General Business** - Mixed industries, cost-conscious, self-serve preference

### Company Size Tiers (5)

1. **Micro** (1-10 employees) - Individual contributors, DIY mindset, <$10K budget
2. **Small** (11-50 employees) - Growing teams, first formal tools, $10K-50K budget
3. **Mid-Market** (51-500 employees) - Established processes, $50K-250K budget
4. **Enterprise** (501-5000 employees) - Complex needs, $250K-$1M budget
5. **Strategic** (5000+ employees) - Custom requirements, >$1M budget

## Key Metrics Definitions

### Price Elasticity of Demand (ε)
- **Formula**: % Change in Quantity Demanded / % Change in Price
- **Interpretation**:
  - ε < -2.0: Highly elastic (very price-sensitive)
  - -2.0 < ε < -1.0: Elastic (price-sensitive)
  - -1.0 < ε < -0.5: Inelastic (less price-sensitive)
  - ε > -0.5: Highly inelastic (price-insensitive)

### Willingness-to-Pay (WTP) Index
- **Baseline**: 1.0 (represents average market WTP)
- **Range**: 0.45 - 3.00
- **Interpretation**: Multiplier on base pricing strategy
  - WTP < 0.8: Budget-constrained segments (require value tiers)
  - 0.8 < WTP < 1.5: Standard pricing
  - WTP > 1.5: Premium pricing opportunity

### Churn Risk
- **Annual churn rate** based on segment characteristics
- **Factors**: Price sensitivity, switching costs, competitive intensity
- **Range**: 2% (Strategic Financial Services) to 22% (General SMB)

### Growth Rate
- **Expected annual customer growth** within segment
- **Factors**: Market maturity, digital adoption, economic conditions
- **Range**: 4% (Mature gov't) to 40% (High-growth e-commerce)

## Demand Curve Parameters by Segment

Each segment follows a **linear demand model**:
```
Q = a - b × P

Where:
Q = Quantity demanded (customers)
P = Price point
a = Market size parameter (intercept)
b = Price sensitivity coefficient (slope = elasticity × Q/P)
```

## Market Condition Modifiers

### Bull Market (+)
- WTP Index: +15%
- Growth Rate: +10 percentage points
- Churn: -2 percentage points
- Elasticity: Decrease magnitude by 0.2 (less price-sensitive)

### Base Market (Baseline)
- All parameters as listed in table

### Bear Market (-)
- WTP Index: -20%
- Growth Rate: -15 percentage points (min 0%)
- Churn: +5 percentage points
- Elasticity: Increase magnitude by 0.3 (more price-sensitive)
